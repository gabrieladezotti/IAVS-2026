IAVS-2026: “The Burning Question”

Author

Gabriela Dezotti

Published

June 1, 2024

1 Objective

This document presents the analyses developed for my poster at the 68th IAVS Symposium. Using data from a long-term experiment in a Cerrado area, I examine how forb community richness and cover change over time in response to fire suppression. The workflow includes data organization, analysis, and figure generation for the poster.

2 Methods

Forb classification follows the document 20260420_GrowthFormsTombador_AFidelis,which lists species and their growth forms for the RNST area. I compare the fire-excluded and mid-dry-season treatments using only rainy-season surveys to reduce the confounding effect of seasonality on community structure. Because the rainy season is the period of highest productivity in the Cerrado, this approach also increases the likelihood of detecting species that are only visible during this period.

3 Data Preparation

I first load the packages required for data handling, modelling, and plotting. These include tidyverse, glmmTMB, DHARMa, ggeffects, extrafont, and emmeans. Next, I recode treatment labels to make figures more interpretable and define the colour palette used throughout the poster. I then read the raw dataset, which contains information on forb cover and species richness through 2016 to 2025.

4 Data Wrangling

Here, I summarise the species-level data by date, treatment, plot, and observer. For each sampling unit, I calculate total forb cover and species richness, then extract year from the survey date and store it as a factor.

Code 
  cover = data |>

  group_by(date, treatment, plots, observer) |>
  summarise(
    totalCover = sum(cover, na.rm = TRUE),
    richness = n_distinct(species),
    .groups = "drop"
  ) |>

  mutate(
    year = as.numeric(substr(as.character(date), 1, 4))
  ) |>
  
  select(-date) |>
  mutate(
    year = factor(year)
  )

4.1 Cover

To test how total forb cover changes over time under different fire regimes, I fit a GLMM with treatment, year, and their interaction as fixed effects. I used a Tweedie distribution with a log link to accommodate the distribution of cover values, and included observer and plot as random intercepts to account for repeated measurements and observer-related variation

4.1.1 Model fitting

Code 
glmm_cover = glmmTMB(
  totalCover ~ treatment * year + (1 | observer) + (1 | plots),
   family = tweedie(link = "log"),
   data = cover
)

4.2 Richness

To test how forb species richness changes over time under different fire regimes, I fitted a generalized linear mixed model with treatment, year, and their interaction as fixed effects. I included plot as a random intercept to account for repeated measurements within plots. I used a Conway–Maxwell–Poisson distribution with a log link to model richness.
